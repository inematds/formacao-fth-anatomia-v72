Lesson 8 · 15 min

The spec sheet for your humanoid

A manufacturer spec sheet, a simulation file, and a measurement don't say the same thing, and sim-to-real lives in the difference. In this lesson you compare two Menagerie humanoids by script, build a spec sheet that separates the three sources, and leave with the study plan for the program.

Goal: compare two Menagerie humanoids by script and build your own spec sheet, separating manufacturer, file, and measurement.

02 · Which robot a lab would consider in Oct 2026

With a price only when there is a source, and always varying by version and reseller. Tap each one.

1. Unitree G1 EDU

It is the platform that shows up in almost everything: Menagerie (29 degrees of freedom), unitree_rl_lab, mjlab, retargeting (GMR), SONIC with open weights, and LeRobot.

The base (US$ 13.5 thousand) has no SDK; the EDU, which does, is on request.

2. Unitree R1

1.23 m, 27–29 kg, from 20 to 40 degrees of freedom depending on the version; US$ 4,900 to 5,900 in the official store (EDU on request).

Fewer people publish a controller for it than for the G1: less ready-made code.

3. Booster K1

95 cm, 19.5 kg, 22 degrees of freedom, 117 TOPS, 1 h 10 min walking at 1.1 m/s (official sheet). In the RoboCup 2026 program, US$ 14,999 is the public price.

The T1, from the same company, has a model in Menagerie (booster_t1, 23 actuators).

4. Open

Berkeley Humanoid Lite: 3D-printed, under US$ 5 thousand in parts (reported), with training in Isaac Lab and sim2sim validation in the repository. ToddlerBot: 30 degrees of freedom, Jetson Orin NX, public parts list.

Both need a workbench and assembly time; the simulation model comes with it.
