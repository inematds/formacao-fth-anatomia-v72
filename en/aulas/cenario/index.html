Lesson 1 · 15 min

Humanoids in Oct 2026: demo, pilot, and production

Humanoid videos are everywhere; robots delivered to paying customers, not so much. Before you open any model, you learn to tell demo, pilot, and production apart with a source and a date, to read a humanoid's price without falling for an old number, and to recognize the platforms a lab can buy today.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The three stages and the evidence each one requires
  2. 2Who is where in Oct 2026: Figure, Atlas, Optimus, Unitree, 1X, Booster
  3. 3Why the G1 doesn't have a single price
  4. 4The 6-robot table and the script that checks source and date

Goal: classify 6 humanoids as demo, pilot, or production, each with a source and a date, without repeating an outdated price or model.

01 · From video to robot in series

Each step asks for stronger evidence. Click to see who is at each one in Oct 2026.

Facts marked "reported" come from the press or an aggregator; those from an official store were read on 10/19/2026.

Mark the lowest stage your source supports. A pretty video doesn't move anyone up a step.

02 · Four traps for anyone reading the market

Click each one.

1. Single price

The base G1 costs US$ 13.5k in the Unitree store, but it comes without secondary development: with no SDK, it's no good for research. The EDU, which is, is sold on request; resellers cite US$ 43.5k to 73.9k (reported).

In the RoboCup 2026 program, the competition version has a public price of US$ 35k. That's three products with the same name.

How to write itBase G1: US$ 13,500, no SDK (unitree.com/g1, read on 10/19/2026). EDU: on request; price varies by version and reseller.

2. Old model

Material from 2024–25 still talks about Figure 01 and 02. Today it is the Figure 03, with Helix 02 controlling the whole body (the low-level controller runs at 1 kHz, according to Figure).

The current Atlas is electric: the product version came out at CES 2026.

3. Vendor that shut down

K-Scale Labs, which sold the open K-Bot, shut down in 11/2025 and open-sourced the rest of the project (reported). Don't recommend it as a vendor.

Cheap platforms that exist: Unitree R1 (R1 AIR US$ 4,900, R1 US$ 5,900), Booster K1 (95 cm, 22 degrees of freedom), Berkeley Humanoid Lite (under US$ 5k in parts, reported) and ToddlerBot (30 degrees of freedom, public parts list).

4. Record as proof

The Beijing humanoid half marathon (04/19/2026) was won in 50:26 by an autonomous robot, against 2:40:42 in 2025 (reported); the World Humanoid Robot Games in August 2026 brought together about 2,056 robots (reported).

A record shows progress in locomotion, not that the robot works in a factory. Market and funding rounds are covered in the VLA course.

"Coming soon" is not a stage, "I saw it in a video" is not a URL, and "2026" alone is not a date. For the Optimus V3, the honest answer is demo, with Tesla's source and the date.

05 · Practice

Everything runs in plain Python: you don't need MuJoCo yet.

E1 · Download the kit and watch the table fail

5 min

Run the "download the kit and check the empty table" block. It creates ~/fth/anatomia and ~/fth/fichas.csv.

Done when: the output ends with 6 robôs, 0 com fonte e data and saída: 1.

E2 · Fill in the 6 robots

15 min

Fill in ~/fth/fichas.csv. Open each robot's source and mark the lowest stage it supports; for the evidence, write one short sentence.

Done when: cd ~/fth && python3 anatomia/kit/conferir_fichas.py fichas.csv; echo "saída: $?" → 6 robôs, 6 com fonte e data and saída: 0.

E3 · A seventh robot

10 min

Add a row with a humanoid you saw this week (video, news, post). Find the primary source and the date before you pick the stage.

Done when: the checker prints 7 robôs, 7 com fonte e data; if you couldn't find a source, the row fails, and that is an answer too.
