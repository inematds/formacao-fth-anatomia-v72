Lesson 5 · 15 min

Feeling its own body: IMU, encoders, force and touch

A standing humanoid is always falling a little and correcting itself. To do that, it needs to know how it is tilted, where each joint is, and how much weight is on each foot. In this lesson you read the sensors the G1 MJCF declares, add the ones that are missing without editing the file, and check the reading against the robot's weight.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Proprioception × perception: what each sensor answers
  2. 2Why the accelerometer at rest shows 9.8
  3. 3Add a sensor with MjSpec, without touching the Menagerie file
  4. 4Ankle force × ground force: the difference is the foot

Goal: read the IMU and the force on each foot of the standing G1 and check that the sum of the ground contacts matches the robot's weight.

02 · Four readings to get right

Click each one.

1. 9.8 at rest

The accelerometer measures the force holding the sensor up, not the motion: at rest on the floor, it reads 9.8 m/s² upward. In free fall, it would read zero.

This is how the IMU knows which way is "down" and, from that, the tilt.

2. Add

sensores.py opens the file with mujoco.MjSpec.from_file, adds an orientation sensor (framequat) on the pelvis IMU and a force sensor on each foot, and compiles.

The Menagerie file stays untouched: you change the robot in your own script, versioned together with your code.

3. 158 × 164 N

The force sensor at the foot site measures what passes from the foot to the leg; the contact measures what the floor pushes back. The difference, about 6 N, is the weight of the foot itself (0.61 kg in the model).

On a real robot, the load cell sits at the ankle for the same reason.

4. Sum = weight

At rest, the sum of the contact forces must equal the weight: 33.3 kg × 9.81 = 327 N. The 1.00 ratio shows the robot is supported only by its feet and with no push.

If it comes out below 1, something is holding the robot up (another part touching the floor, an external force).

Line that checks outsoma dos contatos / peso do robô (327 N): 1.00

03 · Read and add sensors

The excerpt shows how the script adds the three sensors; the block runs for 1 s with the G1 at rest.

Excerpt from kit/sensores.py
spec = mujoco.MjSpec.from_file(str(fth.caminho(MODELO)))
spec.add_sensor(name="orientacao_pelve", type=mujoco.mjtSensor.mjSENS_FRAMEQUAT,
                objtype=mujoco.mjtObj.mjOBJ_SITE, objname="imu_in_pelvis")
for lado, sitio in (("esq", "left_foot"), ("dir", "right_foot")):
    spec.add_sensor(name=f"forca_pe_{lado}", type=mujoco.mjtSensor.mjSENS_FORCE,
                    objtype=mujoco.mjtObj.mjOBJ_SITE, objname=sitio)
m = spec.compile()
Terminal · G1 at rest for 1 s
F=${FTH:-$HOME/fth}; cd $F/anatomia && . $F/.venv/bin/activate
python3 kit/sensores.py --segundos 1
Example from the repository
G1 parado 1 s · 7 sensores (4 do arquivo + 3 acrescentados)
  imu-torso-angular-velocity         [0. 0. 0.]
  imu-torso-linear-acceleration      [0.  0.  9.8]
  imu-pelvis-angular-velocity        [0. 0. 0.]
  imu-pelvis-linear-acceleration     [0.  0.  9.8]
IMU (pelve): inclinação lateral 0.0° · frente/trás -0.1°
sensor de força no tornozelo: pé esquerdo 158 N · pé direito 157 N
contatos com o chão:          pé esquerdo 164 N · pé direito 163 N
soma dos contatos / peso do robô (327 N): 1.00

Gyroscopes at zero, accelerometers at 9.8 upward, tilt near zero, and the weight split between the two feet.

Reading [0. 0. 9.8] and concluding that the robot is moving up is the classic mistake. The accelerometer measures specific force: at rest, it shows the floor's reaction against gravity. To see real motion, compare with the gyroscope and with the pelvis height.
